# Blog brief (the prompt I'm working from)

**Goal:** five long, useful posts at `gameseedassets.store/blog/` that rank for searches
asset-store creators actually make, and that send readers to our assets. They must read like a
small studio wrote them from experience, not like generated filler.

## Who's reading
Indie devs and solo artists who have built something in Unity (a system, a pack, a template) and
wonder whether they can sell it. Some are already publishers with one or two packs and no sales.
They are sceptical, busy, and have read ten "ultimate guides" that said nothing.

## The five posts
1. **How to sell assets on the Unity Asset Store** — the real submission path: publisher account,
   Asset Store Tools, the media sizes (Card 420×280, Cover 1950×1300, Icon 160×160, Social
   1200×630), review, the 70/30 split, pricing. What tripped us up.
2. **How to market your game assets** — what actually moves the needle for a small publisher:
   the store page itself, free assets as a funnel, YouTube, Discord + support, sales/events,
   your own site. What didn't work.
3. **Reddit for Unity Asset Store creators** — which subreddits, their rules on self-promotion,
   how to post so you don't get removed, what to post (GIFs, free packs, breakdowns).
4. **Unity Asset Store vs Fab vs itch.io** — revenue splits, audiences, payout, upload effort,
   pricing freedom. Why we list on all three anyway.
5. **Store media that sells: key images, screenshots and trailers** — using our own covers and
   trailers as examples: what a thumbnail needs at 420 px wide, captioned screenshots, a 45–60 s
   cut, the first 3 seconds.

## Facts: real or nothing
- Every number has a source (official docs/help pages) or comes from our own listings
  (prices, review counts, "views in the past week", our real reviews). Link the source inline.
- Never invent sales figures, download counts, revenue or anecdotes. If we don't know, say so,
  or describe a process instead of a result.
- Our own true facts we can use: 25+ assets, Unity + Fab + itch.io, Code Monkey featured Low Poly
  Fire Particles, free Fog/Rain packs with 7–8 five-star-ish reviews, 3 hours of free support,
  Discord, Arrow Escape at $16 next to a $29.99 competitor, real review quotes, our real media
  files (covers, cards, trailer clips).

## Sounding human
- First person plural ("we"), a specific studio with opinions. Say what we'd do differently.
- Mix short and long sentences. Some paragraphs are one line. No "In today's fast-paced world",
  "delve", "unlock", "game-changer", "elevate", "seamless", "navigate the landscape", "In
  conclusion", "Whether you're X or Y". No em-dash chains. No bold on every other phrase.
- Not every section is a bulleted list. Use lists only where a reader would scan (checklists, specs).
- Concrete over general: real sizes, real prices, real rule text, real file names.
- Admit trade-offs and uncertainty. Let posts end when they're done; no summary-of-the-summary.

## Look and build
- Light (white) reading theme for the blog, same green accents, Baloo 2 headings, DM Sans body,
  same nav/footer as the site so it feels like one place.
- Each post: hero image, reading time, date, table of contents, figures with captions, at least
  two graphics (SVG charts/diagrams made for the post, or our own asset media), a "related
  assets" block, and BlogPosting + BreadcrumbList JSON-LD.
- Generated by `tools/blog.py` from `tools/blog/posts/*.html` (body) + `posts.json` (metadata),
  so new posts are easy. Blog index at `/blog/`, posts added to sitemap.xml and the site nav.
